## Section plan

2.1. Symmetry as the baseline: bilateral in faces, rotational in flowers, translational in architecture
2.2. The symmetry paradox: perfect symmetry is boring; slight asymmetry is more attractive
2.3. Broken symmetry as the source of interest (Noether in physics to meaning in aesthetics)
2.4. Group-theoretic formalization: beauty as a representation of a symmetry group with controlled deformations
2.5. Weyl's Symmetry (1952) revisited: crystals, ornament, music
2.6. The asymmetry budget: how much violation is optimal
